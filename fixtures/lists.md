1. Organize the page around the primary task, not around a catalog of available content. Every major region needs a distinct job.
2. On scrolling pages, put the subject, decision, or primary working surface in the first viewport. On product, venue, portfolio, or object-focused pages, make the actual subject a strong first-viewport signal rather than a small navigation label.
3. On a presentation slide, make one core claim or idea readable at presentation distance and keep its evidence on the same slide.
4. Size headings to their role and container. Reserve display-scale type for a genuine page-level introduction; use compact headings inside operational surfaces.
5. Keep supporting copy close to the visual, control, or claim it explains. Do not make users reconcile distant labels, legends, and conclusions.
6. On scrolling pages, show a hint of the next meaningful section below a first-screen introduction so the page does not appear finished at the fold.
7. Prefer one strong composition over a sequence of interchangeable full-width bands or repeated cards.
